LOCAL MODIFICATIONS TO THE VENDORED DynamicNotchKit
---------------------------------------------------
Five, all small, all marked at the line with a comment saying so. A
re-vendoring must re-apply them or three fixed bugs come back, one becomes
undiagnosable again, and the island vanishes from a monitor with the lid shut.

1. `DynamicNotch.state` is `public private(set)` rather than internal.
   The host's own presentation log and the screen disagreed for six commits and
   there was no way to tell which was right: the controller records what it
   INTENDED, and this is what actually happened. Diagnostic only.

2. `NotchView.compactContent()` gives the two shoulders `.transition(.identity)`,
   EXPLICITLY — not a deleted modifier, which was tried and reverts to SwiftUI's
   default `.opacity` insertion and changes nothing.
   A transition here can be INTERRUPTED and leave the shoulder stranded: laid
   out, reporting its real width, and drawn at opacity 0. That is a window that
   is visible at alpha 1.00, in the right state, at the right size, with real
   content, and blank — which is what "the notch hides" turned out to be. It
   strands when the shoulder's content is LIVE during the conversion inserting
   it — a playing track's 20fps wave, an async artwork load — which is why only
   spoken media commands triggered it. `.blur + .scale + .opacity`, `.opacity`
   alone, and the default all strand; the fault is the interruption, not the
   effect. `.identity` is the only spelling with no wrapper to interrupt.

3. `DynamicNotch.onCompactGeometry`, a nil-by-default host hook called from
   `NotchView`'s two `onGeometryChange` modifiers.
   The island's shape follows its shoulder widths, which live in `NotchView`'s
   private `@State`. A conversion and a content change happening together moves
   the width target while the shape is in flight, and nothing outside the kit
   could observe it. Diagnostic only; costs nothing when unset.

4. `DynamicNotchPanel` is `public` and its `canBecomeKey` consults a new
   `refusesKeyStatus` switch (default false, so upstream behaviour is
   unchanged until the host raises it).
   Upstream hardcodes `canBecomeKey` to true, which makes the panel volunteer
   for every automatic key reassignment — so any attempt to hand the keyboard
   back invisibly (key to an off-screen sink, sink orders out) bounces
   straight back to the panel, and the only remaining release is ordering the
   PANEL out, a visible blink at any moment. The switch makes the panel
   ineligible for exactly the instant of the hand-back. `becomesKeyOnlyIfNeeded`
   cannot do this: it feeds the default `canBecomeKey` this override replaces,
   and was measured changing nothing.

5. `DynamicNotch.notchlessCutout`, a nil-by-default host hook read in
   `effectiveStyle(for:)` and `initializeWindow(screen:)`.
   Upstream's `.auto` turns a screen without a notch into `.floating`, and
   floating has no compact form: `compact(on:)` hides it. With the lid shut and
   the island allowed on a monitor, it therefore showed nothing until something
   expanded it. A size from the host makes that screen use the notch style
   around a cutout of that size. Screens with a real notch never consult it, and
   unset it changes nothing.
